The idea is to shrink the coefficient operations in the early iterations to the width they actually need. In this Kaliski-style loop, CU and CV start as 0 and 1, and each iteration at most doubles max(CU, CV). So before iteration `it` both are at most 2^it, and after the step (CU+CV, or 2·CV) they are at most 2^(it+1). That means all bits at or above `min(n, it+2)` are zero during that iteration.

I restrict both coefficient cswaps and the controlled add to `wc = min(n, it+2)` bits. Swapping or adding zero high bits does nothing, so correctness and the parity-based recovery of the swap bit are untouched. The Bennett uncompute replays the same gates, so it shrinks the same way.

This saves 5 Toffolis per dropped bit, roughly 2,300 per pass and about 4,650 in total. I expect about T ≈ 51,450 and Q = 259 at n=32, so T*Q ≈ 13.3M.
